Loyalty Management System: Defense Cheat Sheet

Panelists judge practical use, not code. For every feature, say who it helps and what problem it removes.

Target length15–20 mindemo included

Opening pitch about 1 minute · problem → who → what we built → what changes

Problem. Darius Poultry Supplies in Caloocan sells pet food, poultry feed and pet medicine. Sales, deliveries, rider cash and regular customers were tracked by hand. The owner could not see who the loyal customers were, how much money was actually collected, or which stock was low or expiring.

Solution. One web system with four parts: a customer shop, an admin portal, a staff portal and a rider portal. Customers earn points on every purchase (1% of the order) and redeem them for vouchers. Every order is tracked from checkout to delivery to the cash being turned in.

Result. More repeat customers, no missing rider cash, and live numbers the owner can act on.

Demo route follow one order, start to finish

  1. Customer shop: browse (point out "Best before"), add to cart, checkout with delivery + COD. customer/products.php
  2. Admin → Orders: new order arrives, confirm it, assign a rider.
  3. Rider portal: mark delivered, confirm with the QR scan.
  4. Remittance: rider turns in the cash, admin verifies it.
  5. Dashboard → Today's Sales → Sales Breakdown: the money now counts, and you can show where it came from.
  6. Loyalty Points: points were added automatically; redeem a voucher.
  7. Analytics → Customer Groups: Loyal, Frequent, Regular, At Risk.
  8. Print a customer statement: one customer per page, full history.

Say these first before the panel finds them

  • Expiration is per product, not per batch or size. "Batch tracking is a future addition."
  • Customer Groups always make 4 groups and are recalculated from current data each time the page opens, so a customer's group can change as others buy. Customers with no purchases yet show as New.
  • Tiers: Silver needs 250 points, about ₱25,000 spent, so most customers are still Bronze. "Tiers reward long-term customers."
  • Needs internet. Walk-in sales can still be entered through Adjust Points or Create Order.
  • Sample data: the @example.com customers and the expiration dates are test data.

Likely questions

How is this different from a POS?
A POS records sales. This also tracks customers, rewards them, follows deliveries and rider cash, and shows trends.
Where is the machine learning?
Customer Groups use K-Means clustering on RFM: how recently a customer bought, how many purchases, and how many points. Similar customers form 4 clusters, named Loyal, Frequent, Regular and At Risk.
Who can access what?
Separate logins for admin, staff, customer and rider. Each browser stays signed in to one account at a time, and every change is logged.
Is customer data safe?
Passwords are encrypted, accounts lock after repeated wrong passwords, and recovery works by email link or temporary password.
Will the owner actually use it?
Show the dashboard's Needs Attention list, low-stock alerts and the printable reports.

When they suggest something

  • Good idea, outside scope: "Thank you, we'll include that in our recommendations for future development."
  • Not needed by the shop: state the limitation once, based on what the shop really does. "The shop only delivers within Caloocan, so…" Don't argue.
  • A real bug they find: acknowledge it, say how you would fix it, move on.
